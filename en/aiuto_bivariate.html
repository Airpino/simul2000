Simulation of bivariate distributions

Pairs (x, y) are generated from a bivariate distribution and we watch the cloud of points take shape, with the equiprobability ellipses, the regression lines and the marginal distributions.

What it shows

Each pair generated becomes a point in the plane; point after point the cloud takes shape, making the joint distribution visible. Over the cloud you can overlay the equiprobability ellipses of the normal, the two fitted regression lines and the histograms of the marginal distributions. At the end of the simulation, a three-dimensional histogram shows the frequencies next to the surface of the theoretical density.

How to use it

The settings screen

  • Bivariate distribution: uniform on the unit square, standard normal with independent components, normal with correlated components (with ρ, suggested 0.7), Dirichlet or bivariate beta (with α₁, α₂, α₃, suggested 1.5; 1; 0.5), defined on the triangle x + y ≤ 1.
  • Total pairs (from 1,000 to 1,000,000) and Update every (pairs): how often to redraw the cloud and the charts.
  • Axis ranges: minimum and maximum of x and y, with suggested values for each distribution.

During the simulation

  • Display: point cloud or frequency map, with the cells coloured according to the number of points.
  • Equiprobability ellipses (for the normals), for p = 0.5, 0.8, 0.95, 0.99, with a table of the fraction of points falling inside each one.
  • Regression lines: of y on x and of x on y, fitted on the generated points.
  • Marginal distributions: histograms of x above and of y on the right, with the theoretical densities.
  • Theoretical support: the boundary of the region where the points lie, for the uniform and the Dirichlet.
  • 3D histogram: at the end of the simulation it opens a three-dimensional view, with semi-transparent columns and the mesh of the theoretical density; rotate it by dragging, zoom with the wheel or two fingers, and adjust the classes per side, the density of the mesh and the opacity of the columns.

Teaching suggestions

Two regression lines

With the correlated normal the two lines, of y on x and of x on y, are different: they meet at the point of the means and form an angle that closes as |ρ| grows. With ρ = 0 they are horizontal and vertical; with ρ close to ±1 they almost coincide.

What an ellipse contains

The table of the ellipses compares the fraction of points falling inside each one with the theoretical probability: with many points the two columns coincide, with few they fluctuate. A link to confidence regions.

Correlation and the shape of the cloud

Repeat with ρ = 0, 0.5, 0.9, −0.9: the cloud goes from a circle to an ever flatter ellipse, while the marginals stay identical. Correlation is visible only in the joint distribution.

The Dirichlet

With α₁ = α₂ = α₃ = 1 the Dirichlet is uniform on the triangle, but its marginals are not uniform: they are beta(1, 2), decreasing. Increasing the α concentrates the points towards the centre of the triangle; with an α below 1 the points crowd along one side.

The three-dimensional histogram

At the end of the simulation, rotate the 3D histogram and compare the columns with the mesh of the theoretical density: with 10 classes per side and many points the agreement is evident; with 40 classes per side the variability of the frequencies in small cells becomes visible.

Technical details

The correlated normal is obtained from two independent normals Z₁, Z₂ by setting X = Z₁ and Y = ρZ₁ + √(1 − ρ²) Z₂. The Dirichlet is generated from three independent gammas G₁, G₂, G₃ with shapes α₁, α₂, α₃: X = G₁/S, Y = G₂/S with S = G₁ + G₂ + G₃; the marginals are beta, X ~ Beta(α₁, α₂ + α₃) and Y ~ Beta(α₂, α₁ + α₃). The fitted regression lines are

y on x: slope Cov(x, y)/Var(x);    x on y: Cov(x, y)/Var(y), i.e. Var(y)/Cov(x, y) in the (x, y) plane

and both pass through the point of the means. The ellipses of the normal with correlation ρ enclose probability p where the quadratic form (x² − 2ρxy + y²)/(1 − ρ²) is below −2 log(1 − p). The three-dimensional histogram is drawn in perspective, painting the faces from the farthest to the nearest. At each node the mesh of the theoretical density shows the mean density over a cell as large as the columns, so surface and columns can be compared; for the Dirichlet, whose density is infinite along the edges of the triangle when an α is below 1, the mean near the edges is computed by double-exponential (tanh-sinh) quadrature, suited to integrable singularities.
