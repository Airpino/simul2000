Mean and maximum likelihood

From each sample the location parameter is estimated in two ways, with the arithmetic mean and with the maximum likelihood estimator, and their sampling distributions are compared.

What it shows

This is the module that in 2003 was still “under construction”. The arithmetic mean is the most widely used estimator of the location parameter θ, but it is not always the best: when the distribution is not normal, the maximum likelihood estimator can be much more precise. The module draws many samples, computes both estimators on each, and compares their sampling distributions, on the same scale, in two overlaid histograms.

The table compares the simulated variances with the asymptotic ones, and their ratio with the asymptotic relative efficiency.

How to use it

The settings screen

  • Distribution, always with location parameter θ and known scale:
    • exponential power (normal of order p), with p ≥ 1: the maximum likelihood estimator minimises Σ|xᵢ − θ|p; for p = 1 it is the median, for p = 2 the mean;
    • normal, the control case: the two estimators coincide;
    • Cauchy, which has no variance: the mean does not improve as n grows;
    • uniform of width 1, the non-regular case: the estimator is the midrange.
  • θ, true value of the parameter; n, elements per sample; m, number of samples (1,000, 10,000 or 100,000); Update every.
  • Histograms: the same range and scale for both, so the comparison is immediate.

During the simulation

  • Single-sample panel: the values of the last sample, the mean (circle) and the maximum likelihood estimate (diamond); the dashed line is the true θ.
  • Histograms, overlaid or side by side, with the asymptotic distributions of the two estimators.
  • Comparison of the two estimators: mean of the estimates, simulated variance, asymptotic variance and interquartile range; below, the ratio of the two variances and its asymptotic value. For the Cauchy, which has no variance, the interquartile ranges are compared.

Teaching suggestions

The median beats the mean

With p = 1 (the Laplace distribution) the maximum likelihood estimator is the median, and its variance is asymptotically half that of the mean: the relative efficiency is 2. The histogram of the median is clearly narrower.

Light tails, heavy tails

Repeat with p = 1, 2, 4, 10: with heavy tails (small p) the median is preferable, with light tails (large p) the maximum likelihood estimator approaches the midrange and becomes much more efficient than the mean. Only for p = 2 is the mean optimal.

The Cauchy

With the Cauchy the mean is useless: its distribution is the same as that of a single observation, whatever n. The maximum likelihood estimator, instead, concentrates around θ as n grows. It is the clearest example of why the choice of estimator matters.

The non-regular case

With the uniform of width 1 the support depends on θ, and regular asymptotic theory does not hold: the variance of the midrange decreases as 1/n² instead of 1/n. Comparing n = 10 and n = 20, the ratio of the two variances goes from about 2.2 to about 3.9: it grows almost in proportion to n.

The control case

With the normal the two histograms coincide exactly: a good way to check that the comparison is correct, before moving on to the other distributions.

Technical details

The asymptotic variances are σ²/n for the mean and 1/(n·I) for the maximum likelihood estimator, where I is the Fisher information of a single observation about θ; the asymptotic ratio of the two is the asymptotic relative efficiency

ARE = σ² · I

For the exponential power distribution in standard form, density proportional to exp(−|x − θ|p/p):

σ² = p2/p Γ(3/p) / Γ(1/p),    I = p(2p−2)/p Γ((2p − 1)/p) / Γ(1/p)

Computing the estimator: for the exponential power distribution Σ|xᵢ − θ|p is minimised with a Newton method safeguarded by bisection (for p = 1 it is the median); for the Cauchy, whose likelihood may have several maxima, the best one is first searched for on a grid around the median and then refined by Fisher scoring (I = 1/2); for the uniform the midrange is used, which maximises the likelihood.
