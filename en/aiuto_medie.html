Module help

Sampling distributions of means

m samples of n elements, with m preferably large, are drawn from a distribution, and we watch how their means are distributed: the central limit theorem at work.

What it shows

From a chosen distribution, m independent samples of n elements each are drawn; the arithmetic mean of each sample is computed, and the m means are used to build a histogram progressively. This shows the sampling distribution of the mean: centred on the population mean μ, with variance σ²/n, and ever closer to the normal as n grows, even when the starting distribution is very skewed.

For each sample the confidence interval for μ is also computed, and the program counts how often it contains the true value: you see concretely what “confidence level” means.

How to use it

The settings screen

  • Distribution of the individual values: the same ten distributions as the univariate module, with the same parameters.
  • Elements per sample (n): from 1 to 1,000. With n = 1 the histogram of the means coincides with that of the individual values.
  • Number of samples (m): 1,000, 10,000 or 100,000. The product n × m cannot exceed 100 million.
  • Update every (samples): with “update every 1” and a low speed each single sample is shown.
  • Confidence level: 90%, 95% or 99%, for the intervals of the single samples.
  • Histogram of the means: minimum, maximum and class width; the suggested values depend on the distribution, on n and on m.

During the simulation

  • Single-sample panel: at the top, the n values of the last sample, their mean (large circle) and its confidence interval; the dashed line is the true mean, the light band the range covered by the histogram of the means.
  • Histogram of the means, with the asymptotic normal curve N(μ, σ²/n); for the Cauchy, the curve is the exact distribution of the means, which is again Cauchy.
  • Histogram of the individual values: on the right, all the values generated, with their theoretical distribution: the comparison with the histogram of the means is the heart of the module.
  • Statistics of the distribution of the means: mean, variance, skewness and kurtosis of the simulated means, next to the exact theoretical values, and the percentage of intervals containing the true mean.

Teaching suggestions

The central limit theorem step by step

Choose a very skewed distribution, for example the exponential or a beta with α = 0.5 and β = 0.9, and repeat the simulation with n = 1, 2, 5, 30. With the histogram of the individual values on, you see the distribution of the means move from the original shape to the normal one, while the theoretical skewness γ₁/√n decreases.

The variance decreases as 1/n

Compare n = 10 and n = 40: the variance of the means falls to a quarter, and the standard deviation, i.e. the width of the histogram, to a half. To halve the uncertainty the sample must be four times larger.

When the central limit theorem fails

With the Cauchy the mean of n values has the same distribution as a single value, whatever n: increasing n does not narrow the histogram of the means at all. The reason is that the Cauchy has no variance.

What “95% confidence” means

With an update after every sample and a low speed, you see the intervals of the single samples sometimes miss the true mean. In the end, about 95% of the intervals contain the true mean. With small n and a skewed distribution the actual coverage stays below the nominal one: a good starting point to talk about Student’s t and the limits of the normal approximation.

Means of discrete variables

With the event indicator the sample mean is the proportion of successes, and its histogram is a binomial divided by n: with small n the individual possible values are visible, with large n the shape becomes normal. The same happens with the Poisson.

The sum of two uniforms

With the uniform and n = 2 the distribution of the means is triangular; with n = 3 it is already bell-shaped. It is one of the simplest and most convincing examples of convergence to the normal.

Technical details

The theoretical values of the statistics of the distribution of the means are the exact ones for the mean of n independent observations:

E(X̄) = μ,   Var(X̄) = σ²/n,   γ₁(X̄) = γ₁/√n,   β₂(X̄) = 3 + (β₂ − 3)/n

where γ₁ and β₂ are the skewness and (non-excess) kurtosis of the distribution of the individual values. The confidence interval of each sample is

x̄ ± z1−α/2 · s / √n

with the normal quantile and the sample standard deviation s: this is why, with small n, the actual coverage is somewhat lower than the nominal one. For discrete distributions the classes of the histogram of the means are multiples of 1/n, the only values the mean can take. The numbers are generated with the same methods as the univariate module.
