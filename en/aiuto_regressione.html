Module help

Simulation of regression relationships

Samples are drawn from a regression model; on each one the two parameters are estimated, and we watch how the fitted lines and the estimates are distributed around the true values.

What it shows

The model is yᵢ = g(xᵢ; β₀, β₁) + εᵢ, with fixed x and random errors. At each sample the y change, and so do the fitted line and the estimates b₀ and b₁. Repeating the draw, the fitted lines “walk” around the true line and the joint distribution of the estimates is built point by point: the most direct way to understand that an estimate is a random variable.

The four windows, as in the original version, are:

  1. samples: all the fitted lines (in green) around the true relationship (in red), with the observed values; the latest fit is highlighted;
  2. pairs of estimates: the cloud of points (b₀, b₁), with the equiprobability ellipses of their theoretical distribution;
  3. sample: the last sample, with the true relationship, the fitted one and the residuals;
  4. text: the simulation plan and the estimates sample by sample; the distributions of the b tab shows the histograms of b₀ and b₁ and the comparison between simulated and theoretical variances.

How to use it

The settings screen

  • Systematic component: linear, β₀ + β₁x, or exponential, β₀ · exp(β₁x); true values β₀ and β₁ (suggested 0.2 and 1.1).
  • Choice of the x: an arithmetic progression (initial x and increment), random (drawn once and then fixed) or typed in, separated by “;”. They can be standardised.
  • Random component: Gaussian errors or exponential power errors, with the scale parameter s. With exponential power errors the Lp-norm regression, i.e. the maximum likelihood estimate, can also be requested, to compare with least squares.
  • Simulation parameters: sample size n, number of samples m (from 50 to 10,000), classes of the histograms of the b.

During the simulation

  • Generate draws one sample at a time: the points of the third window glide to their new positions and the fitted line follows them.
  • Start draws the samples continuously at the chosen speed; Complete draws them all at once; Restart starts again from the initial seed.
  • The windows can be resized and maximised (see the general help).

Teaching suggestions

The walking lines

With the Generate button, one sample at a time, you see the fitted line change at each new draw: it rotates around the mean point (x̄, ȳ), and the ends swing much more than the centre. It is the most effective introduction to the sampling variability of estimates.

Why b₀ and b₁ are correlated

With the suggested x, from 1 to 5, the ellipse of the pairs of estimates is very elongated and tilted: b₀ and b₁ have correlation −0.9. Standardising the x, the correlation vanishes and the ellipse becomes a circle: centring the x makes the estimates independent.

How to reduce the variance of b₁

The variance of b₁ is σ²/Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²: it depends both on n and on the spread of the x. Doubling the increment of the x halves the standard deviation of b₁, like quadrupling n: a cue to discuss experimental design.

L₂ norm and Lₚ norm

With exponential power errors with p = 1 (Laplace), switch on the Lp-norm regression: the blue cloud of the L1 estimates is more concentrated than the red one of least squares, and in the single sample you see an outlier pull the least-squares line much more than the L1 line.

A nonlinear model

With the exponential model and small n the distribution of the estimates is no longer elliptical: it is skewed, shifted with respect to the true values, and some fitted curves diverge. The asymptotic normal is an approximation that improves only as n grows.

Technical details

The estimates are least squares: in closed form for the linear model, with the Gauss-Newton method (with step halving) for the exponential one. The ellipses and the curves on the histograms use the normal distribution of the estimates, exact for the linear model with Gaussian errors and asymptotic otherwise, with covariance matrix

Var(b) = σ² (X′X)⁻¹

where for the exponential model X is the matrix of derivatives at the true parameter values. The ellipses are those of probability 0.5, 0.8, 0.95, 0.99. With exponential power errors the error variance is σ² = s² p2/p Γ(3/p)/Γ(1/p), and the Lp-norm regression is computed with an exact method for p = 1 (intercept as the median of the residuals, slope by golden-section search), by iteratively reweighted least squares for 1 < p < 2 and by Newton’s method for p > 2; its asymptotic covariance is (X′X)⁻¹/I, with I the Fisher information for location.
