Module help

Interactive linear regression

Points are added and moved with the mouse or a finger: the least-squares line and the confidence bands follow them as they move.

What it shows

Unlike the other modules, nothing is simulated here: the data are built by the user. Each time a point is added, moved or removed, the least-squares line, the bands and the results are recomputed instantly. You thus experiment, hands-on, with the influence of individual points on the estimate.

How to use it

  • Adding a point: a click on an empty spot of the plane, which runs from −10 to 10 on both axes; holding the button down, the point can be dragged straight away.
  • Moving a point: drag it.
  • Deleting a point: double-click or right-click; on a phone, press and hold.
  • Example points loads 17 points; New points generates another set; Delete the last one and Restart do what they say.
  • Options: confidence band for the line, band for individual values, level 1 − α, and continuous update: when off, the line is recomputed only when the point is released, as in the original.
  • Results: intercept a, slope b, number of points n, residual standard deviation s, correlation r, R², the t quantile used for the bands, means x̄ and ȳ (also as dotted lines on the chart).

Teaching suggestions

The weight of a distant point

Drag a point close to x̄ up and down: the line moves little, almost parallel to itself. Do the same with a point at the extreme of the x: the line rotates a lot. This is the idea of leverage, better understood this way than with any formula.

The bands widen at the sides

The bands are narrowest at x̄ and widen moving away from it: the uncertainty about the line is smallest at the centre of the data. Outside the range of the observed x, predictions quickly become uncertain.

Confidence for the line and for individual values

The band for individual values is always wider than the one for the line, and beyond a certain point it does not narrow as n grows: adding points makes the line precise, but the variability of individual values remains.

A single outlier

Starting from the example points, drag one point very far from the others: the correlation and R² change drastically. A good way to show that a correlation coefficient should always be read together with the chart.

Building a correlation

Ask students to place the points so as to obtain r ≈ 0.8, or r ≈ 0 with an evident but nonlinear relationship, such as a parabola.

Technical details

With the least-squares estimates a and b, and s² = Σ(yᵢ − ŷᵢ)²/(n − 2), the bands are

for the line: ŷ(x) ± t · s · √(1/n + (x − x̄)²/Sxx)
for individual values: ŷ(x) ± t · s · √(1 + 1/n + (x − x̄)²/Sxx)

where Sxx = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² and t is the 1 − α/2 quantile of Student’s t with n − 2 degrees of freedom, computed with the incomplete beta function. The bands need at least three points with x not all equal.
