Module help

Random number generation

Pseudo-random numbers are generated from a univariate distribution, and we watch their histogram approach the theoretical density, and the sample statistics approach the theoretical values.

What it shows

The module generates values from a selected distribution and progressively builds their histogram, overlaid on the theoretical density. As the simulation proceeds, the histogram takes shape and approaches the curve, and the sample statistics (mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis) approach the theoretical ones: the law of large numbers made visible.

How to use it

The settings screen

  • Distribution: ten distributions, eight continuous and two discrete. For those with parameters to choose, the fields appear right below the list.
  • Total numbers and Update every: how many values to generate in all, and how often to redraw the histogram. The update interval is rounded to a divisor of the total.
  • Histogram: minimum, maximum and class width. The Suggested values button takes as limits the theoretical quantiles of order 1/(2n) and 1 − 1/(2n), rounded to round numbers: so on average about one value in the whole simulation falls outside the histogram. For the Cauchy the range is fixed, because its tails are too heavy.
  • Generator and Colours: as in all the modules (see the general help).

During the simulation

  • Histogram on a variable scale: when on, the vertical scale adapts each time and the histogram always fills the same space; when off, the scale stays fixed and you see the histogram grow.
  • Theoretical distribution: shows or hides the density (or, for discrete distributions, the probabilities) over the histogram.
  • Strip of values: above the histogram, the values of the last block generated, one per tick; the circle is the last value. It conveys the variability of the individual numbers, which the histogram summarises.
  • Sample statistics: simulated mean, variance, skewness and kurtosis next to the theoretical ones, with the 99% confidence interval for the mean.
  • Frequencies and X² test: below the chart, the Show the frequencies button opens the table of observed and expected frequencies class by class, and the result of the X² goodness-of-fit test.

Teaching suggestions

The law of large numbers

Run the same simulation with 1,000 and with 1,000,000 numbers, and compare the two tables of sample statistics: with a thousand times more values, the differences from the theoretical values shrink by about thirty times (√1000 ≈ 32). Skewness and kurtosis converge more slowly than the mean.

A distribution without a mean

Choose the standard Cauchy: the histogram looks like a normal with heavier tails, but the simulated mean does not settle down as more numbers are generated, and every now and then a huge value makes it jump. Many values fall outside the histogram. It is a good example of a distribution for which the law of large numbers does not hold.

The exponential power family

With the exponential power distribution (the normal of order p) one moves continuously from a peaked distribution to a flat one: p = 1 gives the Laplace distribution, p = 2 the normal, and large values of p approach the uniform. Compare the theoretical kurtosis for p = 1, 2, 4, 10.

From the gamma to the normal

Generate from the gamma with α = 0.5, then 2, 10, 50: the theoretical skewness 2/√α decreases and the histogram becomes ever more symmetric and normal-like. The same happens to the Poisson as λ grows.

The X² test under the true hypothesis

Repeat the same simulation several times changing only the seed, and note the P value of the X² test: since the data really come from the theoretical distribution, P should be uniformly distributed.

The shapes of the beta

The beta with parameters less than 1 is U-shaped, with equal parameters it is symmetric, with different parameters it is skewed: for example, with α = 0.5 and β = 0.5 you can clearly see the histogram pile up at the ends.

The sample statistics

The simulated statistics are computed from the empirical moments; kurtosis is the non-excess kurtosis, equal to 3 for the normal. The confidence interval for the mean is

x̄ ± 2.576 · s / √n

with s the simulated standard deviation.

The X² test

Classes with expected frequency below 5 are merged with their neighbours, and values outside the range of the histogram form a separate cell. Since the parameters are known, the degrees of freedom are the number of cells minus 1. Expected frequencies are denoted by Ej, observed ones by Oj:

X² = Σ (Oj − Ej)² / Ej
